Plan coverage and keep dispatch aligned
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will plan coverage and keep dispatch aligned. A paper duty schedule and a dispatch roster can drift apart, especially when members sign up or trade shifts. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
A career department sets up A, B and C shifts, and a volunteer department uses shift sign-ups for weekend duty crews. Shifts show who is on duty and can drive dispatch, so calls page the people who are actually working. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Create an assigned or signup shift with groups and staffing needs. Step 2. Have demo members sign up or complete a trade where supported. Step 3. Review whether dispatch follows groups or shifts and confirm the calendar time zone.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Compare the scheduled roster with the people dispatch would include at the selected time. Check trade completion and the visible duty coverage. A common problem to watch for: A calendar event is not automatically a duty assignment. Shift dispatch behavior must be reviewed on Dispatch Settings.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Schedule a weekend duty crew, change one assignment, and verify the expected roster. Track unfilled slots and unresolved trades. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Schedule mobile-crisis or outreach coverage windows and eligible team roles; review gaps and overlapping assignments.
Search and rescue: Schedule search standby and field-team coverage with eligible roles; do not confuse sign-up with arrival at staging.
Security companies and departments: Schedule site patrol coverage and supervisor positions; review whether members may sign up for multiple shift groups.
Wildland contractors: Schedule crew availability and relief windows; check conflicts with deployments and rest requirements.
Industrial emergency response teams: Schedule facility ERT coverage across plant shifts; review required positions and overlapping assignments.
