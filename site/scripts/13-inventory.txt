Know what is available, issued, and expiring
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will know what is available, issued, and expiring. Unrecorded transfers and missing expiry dates make stock levels unreliable and replenishment harder to plan. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
Medical supplies are tracked by lot with expiry alerts, and turnout gear is issued to members. Know what you have, where it is and what is about to expire. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Define the stock locations and items the department will track. Step 2. Record a receipt, transfer, and equipment issue using sample stock. Step 3. Review counts, expiry, low-stock alerts, and purchasing needs.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Reconcile a physical demo count with recorded stock. Check the destination of a transfer, the equipment assignee, and the expiry record. A common problem to watch for: A purchase order is distinct from a received delivery. Reconcile the receipt before assuming stock is available.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Receive ten demo supplies, transfer two, and issue one durable item. Reconcile the resulting balances and inspect expiry alerts. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Track operational kits and consumables by location; record issues and returns without exposing clinical information.
Search and rescue: Track rope, radios and search kits; issue equipment to a team, then reconcile returns and damaged items.
Security companies and departments: Track radios, PPE and site keys as appropriate to inventory policy; reconcile equipment issued to patrol staff.
Wildland contractors: Track tools, hose and consumables by base or crew; record transfers and returns after an assignment.
Industrial emergency response teams: Track PPE, spill supplies and response kits; review stock, expiry and damaged equipment.
