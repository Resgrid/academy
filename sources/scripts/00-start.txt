Resgrid: from the first alert to the next shift
Audience: All department roles
illustrated lesson; live walkthrough pending

SCENE 1: Ready for the next call
ON SCREEN: A connected operation | People • apparatus • information
NARRATION: Welcome to Resgrid Academy. Resgrid brings dispatch, people, apparatus, and department management into a connected system. This series explains what each feature does, how to prepare it, and how to tell whether it is helping your department.

SCENE 2: One incident. A connected workflow.
ON SCREEN: Prepare people and units | Create and coordinate the call | Review records and restore readiness
NARRATION: Picture a fictional department with two stations, an engine, and a rescue unit. First, administrators organize members, groups, roles, and units. A dispatcher creates a training call and selects the intended resources. Members respond through their assigned devices. After closeout, officers complete records and follow up on equipment, supplies, and staffing.

SCENE 3: Build the foundation
ON SCREEN: Department address and time zone | Members, groups, roles, and units | Permissions and account recovery
NARRATION: Start with the foundation. Set the department address and time zone. Build the roster, home stations, personnel roles, and unit types. Review who can dispatch, manage information, and administer the department. Prepare account recovery as you set up stronger sign-in protections. These choices influence later schedules, recipients, maps, and reports.

SCENE 4: Coordinate the response
ON SCREEN: Call types, priorities, and recipient routing | Status updates and location information | Messages, notification tests, and voice
NARRATION: For operations, learn call intake, types, priorities, recipient routing, run cards, and response statuses. Mapping adds location and site context. Messaging and notification rules keep the intended audience informed. Verify communications on actual devices. A saved call or provider handoff is different from confirmed receipt by a member.

SCENE 5: Keep the department ready
ON SCREEN: Shifts, training, and certifications | Checklists, maintenance, and inventory | Documents, contacts, protocols, and calendars
NARRATION: Between calls, shifts help plan coverage. Training and certification records make learning and renewal work visible. Checklists record routine inspections. Maintenance gives defects an owner and a documented repair process. Inventory records where equipment and supplies are stored, issued, and replenished. Documents, contacts, protocols, and calendars give members a shared reference point.

SCENE 6: Make the back office reviewable
ON SCREEN: Incident and prevention records | Deployment documentation and finance | Reporting, costs, contracts, and billing
NARRATION: Records connect incident reporting, prevention, investigation, evidence, review, and disclosure. Deployments keep extended response activity and supporting documents together. Business Operations adds commercial and costing workflows where enabled. Teach the source activity, review decisions, and recorded outcomes together, rather than treating an export or prepared claim as a completed external submission.

SCENE 7: Availability is part of configuration
ON SCREEN: Check the release and department access | Confirm modules, permissions, and entitlements | Configure, test, and review the observed result
NARRATION: Feature availability depends on the deployed release and department access. Some features need an enabled module, a permission, an add-on, or protection enrollment. The source catalog marks Enhanced AI as planned. Buying an add-on is separate from configuring it. Setup Report helps review configuration evidence; it does not prove that every operational workflow or notification has succeeded.

SCENE 8: Learn by doing
ON SCREEN: Choose your role-based learning path | Practice in a department with sample data | Keep the result and follow-up owner
NARRATION: Choose the learning path that matches your responsibility: administrator, dispatcher, member, or specialist. Each chapter explains a department problem, the relevant features, setup decisions, and a practice task.

ADAPT THIS WORKFLOW TO YOUR DEPARTMENT (written companion):
