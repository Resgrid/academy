Start on the Map tab in Omni IC. It gives the command team a shared view of the department's map. Open Map Layers when you need to see which kinds of information are being displayed.

The switches let you choose which calls, units, stations, points of interest, and department layers you see. The count at the top gives you a quick check before you interpret an empty part of the map.

Turn off Points of interest to reduce clutter. The count drops from six to five active layers. Turn it back on when you need to see saved locations again.

Open the main menu and choose Custom Maps. This takes you from the department map to the site references your department has made available.

Under Indoor Maps, open the Community Coordination Centre. This example is a fictional floor plan. In your department, confirm you have the right site before sharing a room reference.

The Ground Floor tab shows the plan and room names. A search team could use this to agree on a briefing location. A security team could use it to give another shift a precise handoff point.

The tabs at the top switch among the saved floor and data layers. Here, the GIS Import tab is selected. Check the selected tab before describing what the map shows.

Back on the Custom Maps list, open Search Maps. Choose Indoor and search for Briefing. The result includes the site and floor, which helps distinguish rooms with similar names.

Open the briefing room result. IC returns to the matching floor and shows the saved zone name and description. This identifies a place on the floor plan. It does not show who is in that room.

For outdoor coordination, open Weather Alerts from the menu. Use the search box, severity tabs, or sort choices to find the alert you need. These are fictional alerts for a software exercise.

Select the Severe tab. The list now shows only the severe exercise alert. Open it to check what area it covers and when it applies.

Check the effective time, expiry, affected area, and sender. This example is marked Expired. Community outreach teams, wildland contractors, and industrial response teams can use the same checks when planning work around changing conditions.
