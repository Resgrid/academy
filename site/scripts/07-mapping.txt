Make location information useful and current
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will make location information useful and current. A map can mislead when old positions remain visible or useful sites and reference layers are missing. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
A department adds its stations and hospitals as points of interest and lets personnel locations expire after 60 minutes so old positions disappear. A current map shows where responders and apparatus are and puts important locations in front of dispatchers. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Review personnel and unit location-age settings. Step 2. Add stations and hospitals as points of interest. Step 3. Add a relevant map layer, planned route, or weather zone and review its audience.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Check marker age and source, POI placement, reference-layer alignment, and the intended weather-alert coverage. A common problem to watch for: Location sharing requires client permission. Uploaded floor plans are reference material; verify currency and alignment before relying on them.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Follow a sample unit and compare a recent position with a stale position. Record missing POIs and outdated reference layers. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Use current operational team positions for visit coverage; choose a location lifetime and follow the service’s location-access procedures.
Search and rescue: Set a location lifetime suitable for remote search updates; use search-area POIs and distinguish stale positions from current team location.
Security companies and departments: Map patrol vehicles and site POIs; review stale-location lifetime before treating an old position as current patrol coverage.
Wildland contractors: Map staging, divisions and support vehicles; review device update frequency and stale location during remote assignments.
Industrial emergency response teams: Map facility access points, response equipment and team positions; review indoor or weak-signal limitations and fallback procedures.
