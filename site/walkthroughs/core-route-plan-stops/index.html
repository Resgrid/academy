Create a route plan, add stops and verify saved details

Prepare a draft route, set station endpoints, add two located stops with dwell times and contacts, verify the saved plan, and check persistent stop order and the Directions summary.

Audience: Department administrators, Operations supervisors, Route and logistics coordinators

Before you begin

  • Authorized Routes access and a separate fictional training department.
  • An existing station and permitted contacts; verified locations for an actual operational plan.

Chapters

  1. Find Routes from Calls
  2. Name the plan and choose its initial state
  3. Set the profile, radius and station endpoints
  4. Choose a manual stop and its map location

  7. Review the sequence and create the route

Download video · Download captions

Step 1

Find Routes from Calls

From Calls, select Routes in the top action bar. The Routes page lists saved plans and provides New Route beside In Progress and Archived Routes. Begin with New Route for this fictional community supply exercise. A named plan gives administrators one place to prepare the stop sequence and the information needed at each location. Use a separate training department when practicing this process.

Practice

Find Routes from Calls

Step 2

Name the plan and choose its initial state

Enter a recognizable Name and a Description that explains the purpose. Here the name is Community supply loop, clearly marked training only. Keep Unit at Assign at start time, and keep Status at Draft while preparing the plan. These are separate fields on Route Details. Check the department and the plan name before making changes so the exercise stays with the intended team.

Practice

Name the plan and choose its initial state

Step 3

Set the profile, radius and station endpoints

Review Route Profile, the Avoid checkboxes and Geofence Radius. This example keeps Driving, leaves the Avoid choices clear, and sets the route radius to one hundred metres. Check Use Station as Start and Use Station as End, then select Firehouse fifty-one in both station selectors. Optimize Stop Order stays unchecked so we can review the sequence ourselves. The stop list is still empty. Select Add Stop.

Practice

Set the profile, radius and station endpoints

Step 4

Choose a manual stop and its map location

In Add Stop, enter Supply point A as the training Name, choose Manual as Type, and keep Normal Priority. The location area says to click the map to set a location. Use the map controls to find the intended area, then select a point and review the latitude and longitude below it. The Address here is Chicago, Illinois. These are fictional exercise points, so the names do not identify real service destinations.

Practice

Choose a manual stop and its map location

Step 5

Add dwell time, a contact and clear instructions

Scroll within the stop form. Leave Planned Arrival and Planned Departure empty for this exercise, and enter ten for estimated dwell in minutes. Leave Arrival radius blank; the help says a blank uses the route radius. Select Training Warehouse from Select Contact. The contact name and number fill the fields below. Review both, then add the training pickup instruction in Notes. Select Add Stop to place this prepared stop in the new route.

Practice

Add dwell time, a contact and clear instructions

Step 6

Prepare a second stop with its own radius

Open Add Stop again and name the second Manual stop Community point B. Select its own location on the map and check the displayed coordinates. In the lower form, enter fifteen minutes dwell and seventy-five metres for Arrival radius. This explicitly supplies a stop-specific radius instead of leaving the field blank. Choose the fictional Community Centre contact, review its filled name and number, and add the outreach-supplies note. Select Add Stop.

Practice

Prepare a second stop with its own radius

Step 7

Review the sequence and create the route

Review Stops: the count is now two, with Supply point A first and Community point B second. Check their names, Manual type, address and Normal priority before saving the whole plan. Scroll to Create Route and select it. The returned Routes list now contains Community supply loop with Draft status, two stops and the driving profile. This saved row confirms that the plan was created beyond the temporary stop form.

Practice

Review the sequence and create the route

Step 8

Reopen the stored configuration

Select View for the saved plan and inspect its two location pins and the Details panel. The plan still shows Draft, driving and a one-hundred-metre geofence. Select Edit and check the stored Name, Description, Unit and Status. Scroll down to verify both Firehouse fifty-one endpoint selections and the two saved stop rows. Reopening checks what Resgrid retained, rather than relying on the earlier unsaved selections.

Practice

Reopen the stored configuration

Step 9

Change stop order and verify it after reopening

Use the up arrow beside Community point B in the saved stop list. It moves to the first row. Reopen the editor and confirm that this changed order remains. These saved-list ordering actions persist as you use them. For the supply example, restore pickup before delivery by moving Supply point A back up. Reopen once more and verify Supply point A first and Community point B second. Check the actual sequence after every adjustment.

Practice

Change stop order and verify it after reopening

Step 10

Verify saved details and read the directions status

Open Directions from the saved route row and review Stop Summary. Supply point A retains ten minutes dwell, the Training Warehouse contact and its pickup note. Community point B retains fifteen minutes, its Community Centre contact and the delivery note. Above the summary, this example displays Could not retrieve driving directions, with guidance to review the stop addresses or coordinates. Resolve that warning before relying on the plan for road guidance. Saved stops alone do not confirm a drivable route.

Practice

Verify saved details and read the directions status

Step 11

Apply the plan review across department types

The same preparation pattern can organize supply stops for search and rescue, planned security service visits, wildland contractor support, industrial emergency logistics, or community paramedicine and mental health outreach. Fire and E M S teams can use it too. Choose appropriate locations and contacts for your work, allow time at each stop, verify the saved order, and check the Directions result before operational use. A useful plan makes its intended stops and supporting details easy for the next person to review.
