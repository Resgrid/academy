Keep incident and prevention records reviewable
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will keep incident and prevention records reviewable. Incident information becomes harder to review and report when it is spread across calls, attachments, and separate files. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
After each call, the officer completes an incident report from the call and a supervisor reviews it. Keep incident and activity records with the calls they came from, ready for review and reporting. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Review record settings, definitions, numbering, visibility, and retention. Step 2. Create a report linked to a training call and complete its required data. Step 3. Use review, evidence, exports, or submission workflows appropriate to the record.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Check draft and review status, evidence provenance, restricted access, and the actual outcome of any test submission. A common problem to watch for: A queued submission is not acceptance by its destination. Legal holds and disclosure reviews require their owning workflows.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Prepare one complete training incident report and one incomplete draft. Use the work queue to assign corrections and compare analytics scope. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Use an approved operational record definition for visits or responses; select fields and access according to the service’s record policy.
Search and rescue: Record search activity, participants and outcomes; distinguish a saved draft from a reviewed or finalized record.
Security companies and departments: Record patrol or incident activity under an appropriate definition; review required fields and client/site access.
Wildland contractors: Record assignment activity and crew evidence; link the correct call or deployment and retain review status.
Industrial emergency response teams: Record drills and response activity; review the definition, participants and finalization requirements.
