Take an incident from intake to closeout
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will take an incident from intake to closeout. Inconsistent call classification and unclear recipients create avoidable re-entry and uncertainty during dispatch. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
A department adds Structure Fire and Medical call types, sets Responding and On Scene statuses and connects its county CAD email feed. Consistent call types and statuses make dispatching, response tracking and reporting reliable from the first call. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Configure call types, priorities, and response statuses. Step 2. Create a training call, confirm its map location, and choose recipients. Step 3. Review response progress, close the training call, and find it in archived calls.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Check the saved call details and intended recipients. Use a controlled communication test to evaluate delivery separately from the saved dispatch. A common problem to watch for: Run-card recommendations require review. Shift routing, unit crew routing, group routing, and automatic status changes can affect different people.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Create a Medical training call for Station 1. Confirm the location, recipients, status transitions, closeout, and archived record. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Use an approved operational request type for a visit or mobile response; keep clinical triage and sensitive detail under the service’s procedures.
Search and rescue: Use Rescue or a reviewed search type and clear staging information; select the appropriate team.
Security companies and departments: Use Investigation or a reviewed patrol incident type with the correct site and selected resources.
Wildland contractors: Use a reviewed assignment or incident type and staging details; select crew and support resources.
Industrial emergency response teams: Use a reviewed facility incident type and access location; select the facility team and specialist equipment.
