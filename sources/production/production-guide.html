Record the real department workflows

The package contains 24 illustrated, narrated draft lessons, 173 feature recording briefs, and a reference for 336 catalog settings. 7 detailed videos use actual Resgrid screens, with chapters and captions. A local Firehouse 51 stack now supplies real web UI captures and a narrated guided-cut walkthrough. Mobile and remaining provider-dependent workflows still need capture. This is a production foundation, not a release-verified course covering every runtime behavior.

Production approach

Use Firehouse 51 for the captured workflows: 14 fictional character accounts, six apparatus, stations, groups and roles. Every lesson and feature brief has written examples for community response, SAR, security, wildland contractors and industrial teams. Narrated revisions are tracked separately from written companions. Use only synthetic names, contacts, locations, incident narratives, attachments, and protected content. The dedicated local stack is seeded and several workflows are completed in the app. See the captured walkthrough and workflow evidence. Local stack credentials and infrastructure files are excluded from the portable video ZIP.

Each final walkthrough should solve one department task. Start with the operational problem, establish prerequisites, show configuration, complete the task, verify the result, show a common failure, and finish with a practical exercise. Split long chapters into focused 4–8 minute videos after live capture. The illustrated chapter videos are primers; they should accompany the focused screencasts.

Priority recording: configure the department

  1. Open Department Settings. Show the address and time zone, explain their effects on maps and schedules, save, then reopen to confirm persistence.
  2. Open Groups & Stations. Create the two fictional stations and review addresses and membership.
  3. Open Personnel. Invite one sample member and distinguish the invitation from accepted membership. Assign the station and personnel role.
  4. Open the unit types editor, then New Unit. Show Name, Type, Station, and unit crew roles. Save Engine 1 and review the roster.
  5. Review Permissions with separate member and administrator accounts. Demonstrate an allowed action and a denied action.
  6. Open Setup Report, review evidence and unknowns, and use Verify again after saving on the owning screens. Explain that the report does not establish notification delivery.

Priority recording: create and close a call

  1. Open the dispatch dashboard and New Call with a dispatcher account. Confirm the correct fictional department before entry.
  2. Show the source-defined Name, Priority, Type, reporting contact, identifiers, Nature of Call, Notes, and Address fields. Explain which facts the department needs and why.
  3. Use the location tools and inspect the marker. Select the intended personnel, groups, roles, or units using the target release's visible controls.
  4. Explain how Dispatch Settings can expand the audience to a unit crew or group, or use a shift roster. Show the actual result in the demo department.
  5. Dispatch only to the demo audience. Follow the training incident from a member or unit device and demonstrate status updates.
  6. Close the call using the verified release controls, find it in Archived Calls, and show the related reporting handoff.
  7. Use a missing-contact example to separate a saved dispatch from confirmed communication. Record recipient and observed delivery evidence.

Priority recording: failed check to documented repair

  1. Create an approved demo apparatus checklist from a template, assign Engine 1, and set its schedule.
  2. Complete the scheduled occurrence with one failed pump-test item. Show the recorded outcome and follow-up owner.
  3. With Readiness Pro and the Maintenance module available, open a work order and assign corrective work.
  4. Show the configured approval policy, safety hold, part reservation or movement, labor, and vendor charges relevant to the sample.
  5. Record the repair, follow the required approval workflow, and show the resulting hold state and documented return-to-service review.
  6. Compare checklist compliance with the work-order history. Track overdue checks, unresolved defect age, and repair cost separately.

Priority recording: receipt, transfer, issue, and count

  1. Define the sample item and storage locations. Explain durable equipment versus consumable stock.
  2. Create a demo supplier and purchase order, then show a received delivery. Distinguish an ordered quantity from received stock.
  3. Transfer two items to the apparatus and issue one durable item to a member. Show the corresponding destination or assignee.
  4. Perform a controlled stock count, complete reconciliation, and inspect expiry or low-stock alerts.
  5. Reconcile the balances with the known fixture. Track unexplained variance and expiring stock, rather than assuming the database is a physical count.

Priority recording: incident report and review

  1. Start from the closed training call and create the appropriate incident record. Verify the definition and required fields for the chosen department.
  2. Complete source facts and attach a synthetic support document. Show draft versus finalized or reviewed state using the actual target controls.
  3. Demonstrate an incomplete record, a correction, and its work-queue ownership.
  4. Where enabled, show evidence capture, a rubric-based quality review, and a revision comparison.
  5. Use a sandbox destination for external submission; show accepted or rejected outcomes separately from the local saved report.
  6. Review the effects of restricted access, legal holds, exports, and disclosure approval on the sample record.

Capture and release checks

  • Record the deployed version and visible feature gates. Code present in the checkout is not proof of release or entitlement.
  • Validate the English UI labels before narration. The scripts use source labels and require screen review.
  • The current guided video frames native 1280×720 web captures in a 1920×1080, 30 fps master. Future continuous captures should use a readable browser zoom and intentional pointer movement. Capture mobile devices separately and frame them within the landscape master.
  • Natural narration uses Descript's Warm Lulu stock voice, with whole paragraphs for conversational cadence and 0.45-second pauses between scenes. Resgrid is written phonetically as “Rez grid” for speech and remains “Resgrid” in captions. The stock voice has no exposed speaking-rate or pronunciation-lexicon controls. Human listening and terminology review remain pending.
  • Captions use Descript speech-timed cues, corrected to the exact scripts and shifted with the scene pauses. Timing order, complete script coverage, and line lengths are checked before installation. Each spoken cue must also contain audible source audio: a successful Descript export can contain unfinished speech as silence. Raw exports and lesson audits are in production/descript/natural.
  • Check every cut for text legibility, changed state, and any exposed credentials. Show a clear starting state and ending state for every task.
  • Keep a release record for every lesson: deployed version, department role, prerequisites, captured result, reviewer, and revision date.

Rebuild the editable package

Use Python and a local FFmpeg encoder. The package includes clean Warm Lulu narration sources, speech-timed caption exports, original illustrations, and editable scripts. Rendered videos are portable and do not require Python or Descript to play.

python production/descript/render_natural.py --ffmpeg /path/to/ffmpeg --lesson 00-start
python production/library.py

Repeat --lesson for selected installed lessons. Use --all after all 24 clean narration sources are available; a pending voice conversion may not yet have an audio source. To revise speech, generate whole-paragraph audio in the private Descript narration composition, wait for its speech clips to finish rendering, export audio and SRT, and rerender the lesson. Keep spoken and written scripts in sync. The prepare.py, supplement.py, and render.py source files document the original local-voice prototypes and replace course metadata; use the natural renderer for this edition.
