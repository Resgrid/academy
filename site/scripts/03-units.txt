Represent apparatus, crews, and GPS devices
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will represent apparatus, crews, and gps devices. A vehicle name alone does not tell a dispatcher its type, home station, crew, or location source. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
Engine 1 and Rescue 1 are added with their unit types and assigned to Station 1. Units let dispatchers send the right resources and let everyone see which apparatus is available. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Units
Units. Your apparatus, vehicles and response teams. Add each unit with its type and home station group.

Unit types
Unit types. Categories such as Engine, Ladder or Ambulance. Create unit types before adding units so each unit can be classified.

Hardware location tracking
Hardware location tracking. GPS devices that report unit locations. Register each tracking device and link it to its unit.

New Unit
New Unit. Add an apparatus, vehicle or team. List your apparatus or response teams with their types and home stations. Teams that respond only as individuals can mark this module not applicable.

Unit Staffing
Unit Staffing. Assign crew members to units. List your apparatus or response teams with their types and home stations. Teams that respond only as individuals can mark this module not applicable.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Create Engine and Rescue unit types. Step 2. Add Engine 1 with its type, Station 1, and crew roles. Step 3. Assign the crew and, when used, register a tracking device against the unit.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Confirm the unit type, home station, crew, and status. For hardware tracking, check a recent received position and the stale-position behavior. A common problem to watch for: An old GPS position is not a live position. Crew assignment, reported unit status, and physical readiness are distinct.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Create Engine 1 in the demo department and verify the assigned crew from the dispatcher view. Measure incomplete unit records. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Model response areas or outreach teams as groups and mobile response vehicles as units; define team positions appropriate to the service.
Search and rescue: Model bases and field teams as groups and search vehicles or boats as units; define team leader and operator positions.
Security companies and departments: Model clients or sites as groups and patrol vehicles as units; check membership before applying site-scoped dispatch.
Wildland contractors: Model bases and crews as groups and engines or support vehicles as units; require the appropriate driver or crew positions.
Industrial emergency response teams: Model facilities or plants as groups and response vehicles or equipment as units; define operators and response-team positions.
