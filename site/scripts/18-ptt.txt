Set up voice channels for team coordination
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will set up voice channels for team coordination. Field teams need the correct channel membership and working device audio before an exercise or deployment. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
A search team uses its team channel during a training exercise. Coordinate field teams through app-based voice channels. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Voice
Voice. Push-to-Talk voice channels. Create channels and add the members who use them. Availability also depends on PTT. Check access in the target department.

Push-to-Talk
Push-to-Talk. The Push-to-Talk add-on: voice channels in the Resgrid apps. Review seats, supported clients and channel setup. PTT does not provide phone dispatch alerts or certify radio replacement. Availability also depends on PTT. Check access in the target department.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Confirm Push-to-Talk access and supported clients. Step 2. Create a training channel and add its intended members. Step 3. Join from two demo devices and verify transmit, receive, and microphone permissions.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Check membership and actual audio in both directions on the devices used. Review channel access after membership changes. A common problem to watch for: Push-to-Talk channel setup is separate from phone voice alerts and audio-stream configuration.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Run a two-device training-channel check and record which members can transmit and receive. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Plan operational team channels and verify device and add-on prerequisites.
Search and rescue: Plan search-team and staging channels; test approved participants and fallback radio procedures.
Security companies and departments: Plan site and patrol channels with intended memberships; test authorized device access.
Wildland contractors: Plan crew and staging channels; test connectivity and approved fallback communications.
Industrial emergency response teams: Plan facility response channels; test device access and approved fallback communications.
