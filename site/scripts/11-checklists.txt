Record routine checks and missed work
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will record routine checks and missed work. Paper checks make it difficult to see which apparatus was inspected and which scheduled checks were missed. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
A daily apparatus check is scheduled for each engine, and the weekly compliance report shows any missed checks. Recorded checks show what was inspected and what was missed. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Start from a checklist template or build the approved checks. Step 2. Assign the checklist and set its recurring schedule. Step 3. Complete a demo check, record a defect, and review compliance.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Compare due checks with completed, skipped, and overdue results. Confirm defects have an assigned follow-up owner. A common problem to watch for: A completion report documents recorded checks. A failed item still requires action through the department repair process.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Complete a daily Engine 1 check and leave a second demo check overdue. Confirm that the compliance view distinguishes both. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Use a vehicle or operational-kit checklist; follow up on failed items and distinguish scheduled compliance from an on-demand check.
Search and rescue: Use search-kit and vehicle readiness checks; assign failed equipment follow-up.
Security companies and departments: Use patrol vehicle or site-opening checks; review failures and missed scheduled checks.
Wildland contractors: Use crew and apparatus readiness checks before mobilization; create accountable defect follow-up.
Industrial emergency response teams: Use response-kit and equipment checks; review required items and route failures to maintenance.
