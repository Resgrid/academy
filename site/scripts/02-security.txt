Configure a department and protect access
Welcome to Resgrid Academy. In this lesson, we will configure a department and protect access. Wrong time zones distort schedules, and broad permissions expose work to people who do not need it. We will explain the features, the setup decisions, and a practical way to check the result.

A department scenario
An administrator sets the department time zone, hides modules the department will not use, requires two-factor sign-in for administrators and adds a second administrator. Sets the time zone and address everything else uses, and keeps administrative access limited and recoverable. The examples in this series use a fictional department and are teaching scenarios, not default settings.

Department Settings
Department Settings. Department address, time zone and core settings. Confirm the address and time zone; dates, maps and schedules depend on them.

Module settings
Module settings. Turn optional modules on or off for the department. Hide modules you will not use so members see a simpler menu.

Permissions
Permissions. Who can create calls, see personal information and perform other sensitive actions. Review each permission; some default to every member.

Two-Factor Authentication
Two-Factor Authentication. Two-factor sign-in for member accounts. Enroll every administrator in two-factor sign-in.

Security and session policy
Security and session policy. Two-factor requirements, password rules and session limits. Require two-factor sign-in for administrators and review password and session rules.

Single sign-on and provisioning
Single sign-on and provisioning. Let members sign in through your identity provider, with optional automatic account provisioning. Know your department address and time zone, and choose at least two people who will administer Resgrid.

Audit history
Audit history. See who changed department settings and when. Know your department address and time zone, and choose at least two people who will administer Resgrid.

Account sessions and recovery
Account sessions and recovery. Your own sign-in sessions, password and two-factor settings. Know your department address and time zone, and choose at least two people who will administer Resgrid.

Setup help and support
Setup help and support. Setup Wizard, Setup Report and Admin Assist. Know your department address and time zone, and choose at least two people who will administer Resgrid.

Passkeys and account recovery
Passkeys add a device-backed sign-in factor and need deliberate naming, removal, and recovery handling. Enroll from account security on a compatible demo device. Name the credential and review the remaining recovery factors. Confirm an actual sign-in and check the credential list after a controlled removal. Confirm availability in the demo department before recording the live workflow.

Configure one useful workflow
For the training scenario, work in this order. Step 1. Confirm the department address and time zone. Step 2. Choose the modules the department will use and review permissions. Step 3. Enroll two administrators in MFA before reviewing enforcement and recovery.

Verify the result
Now verify the result. Use separate administrator and member accounts to check allowed actions and resource visibility. Test recovery before enforcing stricter sign-in rules. A common problem to watch for: Hiding a module is not evidence that API access stops. MFA enrollment is separate from a tested factor and recovery path.

Try it with your department
Your practice task is simple. Complete Setup Wizard, save on each owning screen, then Verify again. Keep unknown checks visible and assign follow-up owners. Record the result, who owns the follow-up, and when it should be reviewed.

Examples for your department
Community paramedicine and mental health response: Set the local time zone for scheduled outreach visits; review whether a daily reset would incorrectly mark an occupied response team available.
Search and rescue: Choose availability reset rules that suit volunteers and overnight searches; avoid implying that a remote volunteer has arrived at staging.
Security companies and departments: Set patrol-day boundaries and display order; review staffing suppression before excluding an off-duty patrol member from alerts.
Wildland contractors: Review reset times across overnight assignments and the deployment time zone before changing contractor crew availability.
Industrial emergency response teams: Align display time with plant shifts and review automatic resets before overwriting a team engaged in an extended incident.
