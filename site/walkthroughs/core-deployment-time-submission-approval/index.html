Submit a deployment time report and verify independent approval

Submit a saved daily time report, inspect the submitter restriction, approve it as a different manager, and verify locked entries and retained expenses.

Audience: Department administrators, Deployment managers, Time-report approvers

Before you begin

  • Authorized deployment access and two separate permitted training accounts.
  • An existing fictional deployment with a saved draft daily time report and an attached training receipt.

Chapters

  1. Review the intended draft report
  2. Submit and verify the saved state
  3. Distinguish submission from approval
  4. Read the submitter’s review restriction
  5. Review as a separate authorized manager
  6. Verify the approved state and both review times
  7. Inspect the locked entries and retained expense
  8. Check who submitted and who approved
  9. Apply independent review across department types

Watch the walkthrough

Download video · Download captions

Step 1

Review the intended draft report

Begin on the saved Daily Time Report number two for the fictional community support deployment. The report is Draft. Check the crew, incident reference, resource order, request number, cost code and point of hire so you are reviewing the intended assignment. Its notes distinguish personnel travel and coordination from separately recorded resource time. This lesson starts with those saved entries and a training expense already present, then follows submission and independent approval.

Practice

Review the intended draft report

Step 2

Submit and verify the saved state

In Actions, Save and Save and submit are separate choices. Select Save and submit and accept its confirmation for this training report. The returned page shows Saved, the Submitted badge and a submission time. Approved still has a dash. Verify that saved state before handing the report to a reviewer. Submission records that handoff; it does not mean that a different person has already approved the time.

Practice

Submit and verify the saved state

Step 3

Distinguish submission from approval

Notice that Save remains available on this Submitted report. The header fields are still editable at this stage in the recorded example. The submission timestamp and the empty Approved value make the distinction visible. Review the report’s state and the controls actually offered on your screen instead of treating submission as a final lock. The approved-state readback later in this walkthrough will show the different result.

Practice

Distinguish submission from approval

Step 4

Read the submitter’s review restriction

Scroll to Review while signed in as the submitter, Wallace Boden. The notice says that you submitted this time report or have hours on it, so another approver must approve it. Approve is not offered to this account here. Follow that separation of duties by having a different authorized approver review the report. This is a visible restriction in the local training exercise, not an approval that was completed by the submitter.

Practice

Read the submitter’s review restriction

Step 5

Review as a separate authorized manager

The same report is now open under the separate authorized Kelly Severide account. Review the time-entry subjects, entry types, start and end times, breaks and distance, along with the supporting expense. This account is offered Approve in Review. After checking the intended report, select Approve and accept its confirmation. Use an authorized reviewer who is independent of the submitted report, rather than changing the report merely to bypass the notice.

Practice

Review as a separate authorized manager

Step 6

Verify the approved state and both review times

The saved result now shows Approved. Actions lists both the earlier submission time and the later approval time. The Save and Save and submit buttons are no longer present in that panel. The report’s reference fields are greyed out. Check these visible results after approval so the report’s state agrees with the action you intended to take. A timestamped approved report is a different result from an unsaved review screen.

Practice

Verify the approved state and both review times

Step 7

Inspect the locked entries and retained expense

Scroll through the approved report. The existing entry controls are disabled and Add entry is no longer offered. Under Expenses, the saved Fuel row still contains the training receipt description, twenty-four dollars and fifty cents in U S currency, and its attachment icon. The expense-entry form is no longer shown here. The signature fields remain blank. This exercise verifies approval and retained supporting detail; it does not create a contractor or customer signature.

Practice

Inspect the locked entries and retained expense

Step 8

Check who submitted and who approved

Use Back to deployment and review the report row in the Time reports section. Status is Approved. Submitted names Wallace Boden with the submission time, and Approved names Kelly Severide with the later approval time. This index provides a second readback of the separate users. Invoice is blank in this operational-only exercise. Report approval does not, by itself, establish that an invoice, payment or payroll action has occurred.

Practice

Check who submitted and who approved

Step 9

Apply independent review across department types

Use this process wherever your department needs a checked deployment time record. Wildland contractors and industrial emergency teams can review crew duty and resource time. Search-and-rescue, security, community paramedicine and mental health teams can review appropriate deployment activity, as can fire and E M S organizations. Confirm the assignment, submit the report, have a different permitted reviewer approve it, and verify the saved state and supporting detail. Keep that time-report approval separate from the deployment’s own status.
