Pending calls let dispatchers prepare a record before releasing it to selected responders. This walkthrough uses one clearly marked software exercise in a fictional department. Open Calls and choose New Call. Enter a recognizable Name and a concise Nature, then review Priority and Type. We select Routine and Investigation. The location and destination remain blank in this small example; for your own work, complete the fields your department requires and use an accurate, permitted incident location.

Turn on Save as pending, do not dispatch yet. Read the explanation beside the switch. The call will be saved and numbered, with any selected recipients retained as a proposed dispatch. Saving a pending record and dispatching it are separate decisions. This is useful when a dispatcher needs to finish a nonurgent request or review its recipient list before releasing it.

Open Select Recipients and search for Wallace Boden, our fictional member. Check his row and leave Everyone unchecked. The picker shows one selected. Choose Confirm to return that proposed selection to the form. At the bottom, verify one selected, the pending switch and the full Save Pending Call button. Check the exact title and description before choosing Save Pending Call. The member selected here is still part of the proposed list.

The app opens the pending queue with a numbered row. Its narrow columns shorten the name and number, so open the row to read the complete identity. This example is F D fifty one, twenty twenty six, zero zero zero seven. Call Details says Pending, not yet dispatched. Open Dispatched and you can already see Wallace Boden. While the pending banner remains, that tab shows the proposed recipient; its title alone does not mean the call has been released.

Return to Pending Calls and choose Dispatch for that record. Review the proposed member in Select Dispatch Recipients. Here we clear Boden once: the count becomes zero and Confirm is disabled. Select him again and verify one selected, with Everyone still unchecked. Review this final choice before pressing Confirm. This is the action that releases the waiting call to the selected recipient, so use the same care you would when making a new dispatch.

Check the result in two places. The pending queue now says No pending calls. In the same saved call, Dispatched lists Wallace Boden, with Command as his group and User as the recipient type. The pending banner is gone, and Dispatch additional resources is available. We leave that separate action alone. These checks confirm the recorded dispatch state and selected recipient inside the app.

To finish this exercise, check its title and number, then choose Close Call. Select Closed under Close Type and enter a Close Note that identifies the training activity. Turn off Notify everyone on the call for this local cleanup. Read back the type, complete note and switch before selecting the final Close Call button. Close only your exercise record; keep unrelated incidents and their command boards intact.

The console returns to three active training calls, with zero pending and zero scheduled. Search for our exercise name: the call panel shows No active calls while the department total remains three. A warehouse search still finds the original incident. Our exercise record also remains readable in Call Details, with its Close Call action removed. Clear the search when finished. The same prepare and review pattern can serve fire and EMS, community care and mental health teams, search and rescue, security, wildland contractors, and industrial teams. This browser exercise confirms these application records; it does not establish notification delivery, a physical response or native device behavior.
