In Responder, open Shifts from the navigation, then choose Today. Across the top are My Shifts, Calendar, Trades, On Duty and Approvals. Start by reading the intended day card. Here, A Shift at Firehouse 51 runs from eight in the morning until eight the next morning. The words next day matter when planning an overnight handover. Assigned describes this schedule's assignment mode. You are on identifies the signed-in person's roster membership, and Fully staffed is the displayed coverage result.

Use Search shifts to narrow the list. Entering Community leaves the Community standby signup card. Read its date and its eight-to-noon time range. This card shows Sign-up and Approval required, while the current account already shows You are on. Those labels answer different questions: how this shift is offered, whether approval is required, and whether this person is already on its roster. Searching changes what you see in the list; it does not change the saved assignment.

Open the standby card to read its day detail. The heading and date identify the selected occurrence, and Scheduled for repeats its time range. The page explicitly says that sign-ups and trades on this shift need supervisor approval. Read that rule before planning a coverage change. Open needs shows Firehouse 51 as Filled, with No role requirements for this particular day. Request trade and Add person are visible actions here. This walkthrough is inspecting the saved day, so neither action is submitted.

Scroll through Roster and compare the person with the group and role beside them. Wallace Boden appears under Command with Chief, while the Engine and Squad entries identify their own people and roles. The repeated Scheduled label describes the saved roster source. Use these details to check that the people and functions you expected are represented. Confirm actual availability and attendance through your department's procedures before assigning work based on this schedule.

Return to the shift views and choose My Shifts. Under Shifts I am on, the A Shift summary shows its code, personnel count, group count, next displayed day, rotation and Assigned label. Scroll to the standby summary to compare its own code and time range. It shows Manual, Sign-up and Approval required. Upcoming days begins a separate section below these summaries. Keep the shift definition and the dated occurrence distinct when checking which day you need to open.

Choose Calendar to look beyond the current day's list. The month heading establishes the period you are reading, and the filter row offers All Shifts or an individual shift. Select Training, Academy signup practice, then select the twenty-seventh of October. The legend identifies My shifts, Open slots and Fully staffed. The selected date appears above its day card, which shows the practice shift from one until three in the afternoon with two open slots. This recorded date is historical; its open-slot label does not establish a current signup opportunity.

Open that practice day to read the actual group requirements. Open needs shows Firehouse 51 with Open, two, and a Chief requirement marked Needed, two. Below it, Roster says No one is on this day yet. Compare the required function with the saved roster before deciding how to fill a gap. Add person is visible, but this inspection does not change the roster. Checking the dated requirement helps a supervisor distinguish an uncovered function from a day that simply has a familiar shift name.

Return to the views and choose On Duty. A Shift shows ten on-duty entries in this recorded state. Each entry gives a name, group and role, an Until time and a Scheduled source label. Read the shift heading with the individual row so you do not mix two overlapping schedules. This view makes the saved coverage picture easier to review. Its Scheduled entries still need the appropriate availability and attendance checks for your department's real work.

Choose Trades to review the trade workspace. This account currently shows No shift trades, and explains that requests you send or receive appear here. Then choose Approvals. The recorded result is Nothing to approve, with a note that sign-ups and trades waiting for your approval appear here. These are the current account's displayed queues. An empty queue is not a completed approval or a confirmed replacement. Read the actual pending entry and its result whenever your department performs those operations.

Return to the intended day and recheck its identity, hours, personal roster label and approval requirement. The same checks help community paramedicine and mental health teams plan outreach coverage, search-and-rescue teams review mission support, security companies check a staffed post, wildland contractors plan crew coverage, and industrial response teams review an emergency roster. Fire and EMS departments can use them for their own station coverage. Match your department's roles and dates, investigate gaps, and verify the actual people before relying on a schedule for a handover.
